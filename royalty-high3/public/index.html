HOW TO PLAY

The Goal

Royalty High is a card game for two. Build units on your side of the board, raid your opponent's side, and try to still have your Royals (J/Q/K) standing when the deck is used up. Whoever has the most Royal points on their board at the end wins.

The Deck (54 cards)

  • 40 Knights — A and 2 to 10, four of each. There are no suits.
  • 12 Royals — each one is rolled on its own when the deck is built: roughly a third Jacks, a third Queens and a third Kings, with a 0.7% chance of an Emperor (super rare, worth 4 points!). The mix is different every game.
  • 2 Jokers — wild.

Setup & Mulligan

Both players start with 5 cards. Before play begins you can mulligan once: your hand is shuffled back into the deck and you draw a fresh 5. You don't draw on your very first turn.

Your Turn

Every turn after your first begins with drawing a card. Then pick a path:

  • Path A (default): take 1 action.
  • Discard 2, Put 2: throw away 2 cards from your hand and take 2 actions instead.

Path B is for putting lots of units down at once. It's deploy-only — no attacking on a Path B turn.

Actions

  • Deploy: place cards face-down at the left or right end of your board. Same-rank cards played together become one unit. Bonus: while your board has fewer than 3 units, deploying is free — keep placing units until you hit 3 (or press "Done Deploying"), and only then is the action used up.
  • Attack: throw same-rank cards from your hand at the left or right end of your opponent's board. Attacking ends your turn straight away, and you can't do it on a Path B turn.
  • Pass: give up your remaining action and end the turn.

Units & Power

Same-rank cards combine into one unit and their power adds up: two 6s hit for 12, three 4s for 12. Royals can be stacked too, but they have no power — a Royal always takes out exactly one defender (see below).

Combat

Your attacking unit meets the opponent's units one at a time, starting from the end you chose.

Knight vs Knight

  • If you have more power, the defender dies and whatever power is left over keeps going into the next defender.
  • If you have less power, you're blocked: your unit dies and theirs lives.
  • If it's equal, both die.

Royal attackers

A Royal (J/Q/K) ignores power: it removes exactly one defender — whatever it is — and dies too. Perfect for deleting something scary.

Example: Royal attack on a single knight
Board: 9, 6, J. A King hits the left end — the 9 is gone and so is the King. The 6 and J are fine.

Board: 8+8 (one unit, 16 power), 6, J. A King hits the left end and wipes out the whole 8+8 unit — both cards — then dies. That's why Royals are great against big stacks.

Attacking a Royal defender

Royals are deadly on defense. Anything that hits one — knight or Royal — dies along with it.

Example: Knight hits a Royal
Board: Q, 5, 3. A 10 hits the left end, runs into the Queen, and both are destroyed. The 10 can't get past.

The Ace Trap (very important!)

On defense an Ace looks harmless (power 1), but it's a trap. Whatever attacks it — knight or Royal — gets captured: instead of dying, your attacking cards switch sides and become a unit on your opponent's board, right where the Ace was. The Ace itself is used up.

So attacking an Ace is usually a disaster — you lose the unit and your opponent gets it. Be extra careful with Royals: you'd be handing over points.

Example: Ace traps a Knight
Board: Ace, K, 4. An 8 hits the left end — the Ace springs and the 8 now sits on the defender's board where the Ace was.
AttackerDefender's board
Example: Ace traps a King
Board: Ace, Q, 6. A King hits the left end — the Ace is spent, and now the King scores 3 points for the defender!
AttackerDefender's board

Two Aces in one unit still only spring once, and both are used up.

Jokers (Wild Cards)

There are 2 Jokers. A Joker can join any group you deploy or attack with and counts as that rank.

  • Joker alone: fights like a Royal (removes one defender) and scores 2 points at the end.
  • Joker + Knight: copies the knight's number and still scores 2 at the end. Joker + 7 = 14 power, 2 points. Joker + 7 + 7 = 21 power, 2 points.
  • Joker + Royal: becomes another copy of that Royal, for fighting and scoring. Joker + King = King + King (6 points). Joker + Emperor = 8 points.

Hand Limit

You can hold at most 10 cards. If you're over 10 when your turn ends, you discard down to 10.

End of Game

When the deck is empty, the Forced Attack phase begins. Each turn you must either attack or press Pass & Discard All to throw away your whole hand. Once neither player has any cards in hand, the game ends.

Every unit is flipped over, and the Royals and Jokers still on your board score:
